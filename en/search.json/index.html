The 2026-08-14 snapshot gives a mixed answer: Stellar ranks ninth in stars, but third in monthly npm downloads; its star growth slowed after a strong 2024, yet its recent annual growth is still ahead of several peers. TL;DR: three conclusions Positioning is out of step with the mainstream. Stellar’s four integrated content systems are unusually complete, but users looking for a lightweight blog may perceive them as complexity. Growth has no flywheel. System-level releases create discussion and tutorials; after the January 2024 release burst, March added 236 stars in one month. When comparable events stopped, growth returned to roughly 30 stars per month. Trust and conversion are leaking. A 13-month release gap weakened confidence. Stellar has many installers but fewer public endorsements, and Chinese-first materials limit overseas conversion. The practical sequence is to clarify the positioning first—a blog and knowledge base in one—provide a low-friction starting path, then turn 2.x into an event and establish a monthly maintenance rhythm. Product review: positioning and mental models Theme One-line positioning Mental model NexT Classic, elegant, and extensible The established default Butterfly Card-based and feature-rich The Chinese community favorite Volantis Modular and highly customizable The modular all-rounder Redefine Simple, fast, and clean Minimalist design Solitude Designer-oriented with distinctive pages A personal homepage with flair Stellar Business-like minimalism plus four content systems A knowledge-management blog The mainstream Hexo need is a lightweight blog: posts, categories, tags, links, and comments. Most leading themes answer “how can a blog look and work better?” Stellar answers “what else can live beside a blog?” It treats blog, knowledge base, topics, and notebooks as one content system. That is valuable for long-term creators, but heavy for users who only want to publish posts. Do the four systems really have no competitors?That claim needs correction. Volantis officially supports collaboration and documentation modules, so it overlaps with Stellar as an all-rounder. Stellar’s actual difference is the integration of four systems and dynamic data components out of the box, without assembling modules. Redefine has notebooks and Solitude has short-form posts, so multiple content types are not unique to Stellar; few themes package them as a coherent system. At the engineering level, Stellar has built-in search while most peers require a search plugin. Pjax is common among competitors but not built into Stellar, and Solitude is the only one in this group with built-in PWA support. Stellar leads in content systems, not in every aspect of engineering convenience. The double edge of substantial features Environment cost: Stellar requires Node ≥ 22, while several peers support older Node versions. Cognitive cost: topic, wiki, and notebooks introduce concepts that require documentation before their value becomes obvious. Migration cost: moving from Butterfly or NexT changes the content model, not just the visual skin. Inference: the real cost is not “cannot install,” but the first impression. A lightweight theme gives an immediate reward; Stellar asks users to understand its positioning first. The answer is not to remove depth, but to explain the cost clearly and prove that a useful site can be running in 30 minutes. Knowledge management is not yet a Hexo categoryBlogging is a mature category in Hexo; knowledge bases and documentation sites are not. Stellar is betting on a real but smaller audience: creators who want to manage knowledge inside their blog. Category awareness comes from repeating one sentence across the README, documentation, showcase, and tutorials: a blog and knowledge base in one. Undervalued assets npm adoption: third-place monthly downloads suggest that people who discover Stellar are willing to use it. Integrated content systems: the combination of four systems and dynamic data components remains a strong differentiator. Documentation and examples: the Wiki, blog/docs example repository, showcase sites, and community provide a solid foundation. The issue is not that these assets are weak; they have not yet been systematically converted into reach, stars, and community participation. Conclusion: mismatch, not only growthThe complete conclusion is twofold: positioning mismatch and insufficient growth mechanisms. Stellar serves knowledge-management creators while the mainstream expects lightweight blogs; event-driven growth stops when major events stop; long release gaps consume trust; adoption does not yet become advocacy; and users do not consistently flow back into the community. Positioning is the direction and growth is the amplifier. The order is to explain the positioning, then amplify it through releases, examples, and a steady maintenance cadence.
